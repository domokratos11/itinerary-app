Three light and three dark themes to choose from

Six new palettes, grouped as three families that each have a day and a night side, shown next to the theme the app uses today (Sen). Every preview is drawn with the app's real token names and fonts, and the controls copy the shapes of the app's own components (filled fields, pill tabs, 14px corners), so what you see is close to what a swap of values in globals.css would give. It is a hand-drawn copy, not the app itself, and it packs every colour onto one screen, so it looks busier than any single screen of the app. Nothing in the app has been changed.

What stays the sameThe light and dark switch, the layout, fonts, corner radius and every component.
What a theme changesOnly the 30 or so colour values behind the tokens: surfaces, text, primary, the "now" marker and the status colours.
City coloursEach city's line colour comes from the trip's own data, so the samples (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka) are the same in every theme.
Mixing is allowedA light theme from one family can be paired with a dark theme from another.

What to weigh when choosing

Primary against the city linesSen's indigo sits close to a blue city line. Kumo and Sumi have no hue of their own, so the city colours carry all the colour.
"Now" against "delete"In Sen, Kumo and Matsu the "now" orange is near the destructive red. Ume and Yoi move "now" to teal, which cannot be mistaken for an error.
Green families and "success"Matsu and Mori use a green primary, so "paid" and "booked" are shifted to a yellower green to stay distinct.
Cost of a swapValues only, in two blocks of globals.css. The status colours, charts and sidebar all read the same tokens.
